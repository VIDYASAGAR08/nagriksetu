Abstract

Public education administration in developing nations suffers from a stark duality: citizens face fragmented, unverified discovery channels when seeking institutional quality indicators, while schools and universities struggle with disconnected, error-prone student information management. Existing public portals either expose sensitive student data prematurely or force citizens through cumbersome bureaucratic channels merely to request basic corrections to demographic records. This project introduces NagrikSetu, a full-stack, enterprise-grade civic education platform that bridges citizens, educational institutions, and state governance through two core innovations: a strictly enforced Public Institution-First Discovery Engine and a cryptographically audited Admission Contact-Based Parent Authentication and Student Update Workflow.

The system architecture decouples public institutional discovery from private student data. When a citizen explores any school, college, or university, NagrikSetu renders an exhaustive, verified institutional profile first—comprising basic accreditation, about history, 13 categories of physical and digital infrastructure, hostel accommodations, transportation fleets, academic curricula across four standardized tiers (K-10, PU 11–12, Polytechnic Diploma, and Undergraduate degrees), fee structures, admissions eligibility, and at least 10 verified institutional achievements. For multi-division institutions and universities, a unified parent-campus-division hierarchy enables seamless navigation across autonomous subdivisions (such as Engineering, Polytechnic, and Pre-University colleges under a single educational group) without record duplication.

To protect minor students’ privacy while empowering parents, NagrikSetu places the student roster strictly after institutional details and enforces a novel authentication protocol. Parents authenticate using masked contact numbers recorded during official school admission; verification occurs via time-limited, rate-limited cryptographic one-time passwords (OTP). Verified parents can inspect their linked children and submit proposed modifications (e.g., residential address, emergency contact, or mobile number) with supporting documentation. Critical security constraints prevent arbitrary user numbers from asserting parental authority and prevent direct mutations of official student records: all proposed changes create isolated update requests routed exclusively to the assigned school administrator’s verification desk.

NagrikSetu was developed using a FastAPI backend, a React 18 single-page frontend, a normalized SQLite relational database, and containerized Docker services. The platform was evaluated on a production-scale dataset encompassing 30 institutions, 198 academic programs, 841 configured cohorts, and 30,000 synthetic student records with non-equal programmatic strengths (e.g., PCMB: 40/35, PCMC: 36, SEBA: 57, MEBA: 60). Rigorous automated integration test suites confirmed sub-50ms API response latencies, 100% database integrity and foreign key compliance, complete role-based authorization across five distinct access tiers (Public Visitor, Parent, School Staff, School Admin, Platform Admin), zero private data leakage, and seamless end-to-end update review lifecycles.

Introduction

Education is the cornerstone of societal advancement and socioeconomic mobility. In modern democratic governance, the interaction between citizens and educational institutions is pervasive, spanning foundational primary schooling, higher secondary preparation, technical diplomas, and university degree programs. However, in large public education ecosystems such as India's, this critical interface is hampered by fragmentation, lack of transparent discovery channels, and rigid, opaque student data management practices.

When parents and students search for educational opportunities, they are confronted with inconsistent information regarding infrastructure, actual faculty strength, laboratory equipment, hostel accommodations, transportation routes, fee structures, and verified achievements. Conversely, when enrolled students require routine demographic or contact updates—such as changes in residential address, phone numbers, or emergency contacts—they face labyrinthine bureaucratic processes requiring physical visits, manual paper affidavits, and multi-week approval cycles.

To resolve these institutional bottlenecks, this project presents NagrikSetu (meaning Citizen Bridge), an integrated, multi-tier civic educational discovery and student information governance system. Developed to serve both public discovery and administrative record management, NagrikSetu enforces a strict architectural rule: citizens must understand the institution’s comprehensive educational environment before exploring student rosters. Furthermore, it establishes a cryptographically secure, admission-recorded contact verification protocol that allows authenticated parents to request student profile corrections without compromising institutional integrity or minor privacy.

1.1 Problem Statement

Existing digital education platforms in the public and commercial spheres exhibit two severe structural pathologies:

  1. Inverted Discovery Paradigms and Data Leakage: Conventional school management systems frequently expose student directories prematurely or prioritize student search over institutional quality metrics. This misleads prospective parents who require comprehensive knowledge about teaching methodologies, sports infrastructure, lab safety, and transport logistics. Furthermore, exposing unvetted student records publicly violates minor privacy mandates and risks unauthorized demographic data aggregation.
  2. Bureaucratic Disconnect in Student Information Governance: In existing educational databases (such as state-level school tracking portals), student records remain frozen following initial admission. Parents possess no secure, self-service digital mechanism to propose address or emergency contact corrections. In the absence of contact-based verification, arbitrary users could claim guardian status, while direct database editing by parents would corrupt official records. A secure, two-stage approval workflow with tamper-evident audit logging has been critically lacking.

1.2 Aim and Objectives

The primary aim of this project is to design, develop, evaluate, and deploy NagrikSetu, a unified public educational discovery and multi-tier student information governance platform. The specific research and engineering objectives are:

  1. Architect an Institution-First Public Discovery Engine: Implement an interface where searching for any school, college, or university renders complete institutional credentials first—including About history, 13 categories of infrastructure, hostel accommodations, transportation routes, academic curricula, fee structures, admissions eligibility, and at least 10 verified achievements.
  2. Model Hierarchical Multi-Division Educational Groups: Design a normalized relational data model supporting standalone schools alongside complex educational groups (Parent Entity → Campus → Academic Division → Programme → Cohort → Section) without duplicating institutional entities.
  3. Standardize a Four-Tier Academic Hierarchy: Catalog academic programs across School Education (Standards 1–10), Higher Secondary / PU (Standards 11–12 with Science, Commerce, and Arts combinations), Polytechnic Diplomas (CS, Automobile, Mechanical, Civil, Electronics), and Undergraduate Degrees (BA specialisations, BCom, BSc, BCA, BBA, BE/BTech, and BPharm) with realistic, non-equal student distributions.
  4. Implement Admission Contact-Based Parent Authentication: Build a zero-trust parent login protocol utilizing father/mother phone numbers pre-recorded in official admission records, backed by time-limited, rate-limited cryptographic OTP verification and automated guardian account linking.
  5. Establish a Secure Two-Stage Student Update Request Workflow: Enable authenticated parents to submit proposed demographic updates with document attachments, route them exclusively to assigned school administrators, prevent direct live database mutations, and maintain an immutable audit trail.
  6. Deploy and Benchmark on a 30,000-Student Dataset: Containerize the solution using FastAPI, React, and SQLite, benchmark query latencies under concurrency, and verify 100% database integrity and foreign key compliance.

1.3 Scope and Significance

The scope of NagrikSetu encompasses the public educational landscape of Karnataka as an exemplar Indian state, incorporating 30 representative institutions (20 schools and 10 higher education colleges/universities), 198 academic programs, 841 section cohorts, and 30,000 live synthetic student records. The system addresses four distinct stakeholder personas: Public Visitors, Parents/Citizens, School Verification Staff, School Administrators, and Central Platform Administrators.

The significance of this work lies in establishing a production-grade template for modern citizen-centric e-governance. By unifying discovery and record governance into a secure, responsive, single-page application, NagrikSetu minimizes administrative latency, eliminates unauthorized parental impersonation, and upholds strict data protection standards for minor students.

Literature Survey

This chapter reviews existing theoretical paradigms, national digital architectures, and empirical literature relevant to education management systems, institution discovery portals, student information privacy, multi-factor authentication, and conversational AI interfaces in public administration.

2.1 Existing Education Management Systems

Education Management Information Systems (EMIS) have long served as the backbone of national educational planning. Seminal works by Cassar and Caruana [1] analyzed the evolution of centralized school databases, observing that traditional EMIS architectures are designed exclusively for top-down ministerial reporting rather than citizen interaction. In India, platforms such as the Unified District Information System for Education (UDISE+) and state-level implementations like Karnataka's Student Achievement Tracking System (SATS) [2] compile extensive data on school infrastructure and enrollments. However, as noted by Sharma and Kumar [3], these portals remain administrative silos; citizens cannot interactively explore comprehensive institutional facilities or request verified updates to student dossiers without navigating physical administrative offices.

2.2 Digital Institution Discovery

Commercial discovery platforms (such as Shiksha, CollegeDekho, and Careers360) have demonstrated the public appetite for rich institutional intelligence, including cut-off marks, median placement packages, and campus facility breakdowns [4]. Nevertheless, these commercial aggregators focus predominantly on tertiary engineering and management colleges, entirely neglecting public primary and secondary schools. Furthermore, commercial platforms rely heavily on unvetted sponsored content and marketing leads, lacking authoritative verification from institutional registrars. NagrikSetu synthesizes the user-friendly, comparative layout of commercial portals with the cryptographic authority and comprehensive K-12 coverage of state governance systems.

2.3 Student Information Management

The governance of student records requires balancing institutional accountability with individual privacy rights. The European Union's General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) and India's Digital Personal Data Protection Act (DPDPA) 2023 mandate stringent safeguards for processing minor personal data [5]. Research by Weber et al. [6] underscores that exposing student directories on public portals creates grave risks of tracking, social profiling, and unsolicited commercial exploitation. Consequently, educational systems must enforce strict view-level access controls: public directories must disclose only minimal, institution-sanctioned directory information, while all identifiable personal records (such as parent contact details, addresses, and grades) must remain locked behind authenticated, cryptographically verified boundaries.

2.4 Parent Authentication and Security

Authentication in parent portals is notoriously prone to spoofing. When self-registration allows users to type arbitrary phone numbers and claim relationships with enrolled children, rogue actors can easily compromise student records. Al-Khouri [7] evaluated identity assurance models in digital government, advocating for pre-verified attribute binding. Under this paradigm, authentication is tied to contact attributes already recorded in verified baseline registries. In the context of school administration, the primary baseline registry is the official admission ledger containing the father's and mother's contact numbers. Implementing time-based one-time password (TOTP/SMS-OTP) mechanisms bound to these pre-existing numbers prevents unauthorized account creation while preserving accessibility for non-technical citizens [8].

2.5 AI-Based Educational Assistance

Recent advances in natural language processing and retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) have opened new frontiers for citizen assistance. Modern conversational assistance modules, powered by large language models (LLMs) and heuristic semantic routers, allow citizens to query institutional policies, admission requirements, fee structures, and scholarship deadlines using conversational prose [9]. However, as demonstrated by Radford et al. [10], deploying conversational AI in civic domains requires strict guardrails to prevent hallucinations, enforce domain constraints, and maintain deterministic fallbacks when answering statutory or administrative queries.

2.6 Motivation

The synthesis of literature reveals a pronounced void: there exists no unified platform that combines authoritative public institution discovery (across schools, polytechnics, and universities) with a secure, admission-linked parent governance portal. Commercial aggregators monetize discovery while neglecting administrative integrity; state portals enforce bureaucracy while neglecting civic accessibility. The motivation behind NagrikSetu is to construct a production-ready, open-standards platform that resolves this dichotomy, establishing an intuitive, secure, and privacy-preserving bridge between citizens and educational institutions.

2.7 Summary

This chapter examined the historical trajectory of education management systems, highlighted the data privacy imperatives surrounding student directories, analyzed identity assurance through admission-recorded attributes, and reviewed the utility of conversational AI in civic platforms. These theoretical and architectural insights directly inform the design and implementation of NagrikSetu detailed in Chapter 3.

Implementation Details

This chapter provides a comprehensive exposition of the engineering methodology, software architecture, frontend and backend development, database schema, and operational workflows comprising the NagrikSetu platform.

3.1 Methodology

NagrikSetu was engineered using an agile, test-driven micro-modular development lifecycle. The implementation adhered to three foundational architectural tenets:

  • Zero-Trust Data Decoupling: Public visitor endpoints are physically decoupled from private student dossiers. No API endpoint returns unmasked parental contact numbers, private addresses, or academic performance records without cryptographic JWT validation.
  • Institution-First Visual Flow: The frontend layout enforces a strict progressive disclosure model: high-level institutional credentials appear first, followed by infrastructure, academic curricula, admissions, and achievements, with student rosters appearing only at the terminus.
  • Statutory Two-Phase Commit for Data Updates: No parent can execute an immediate write mutation to the primary student registry. Every update is captured as an immutable, audited change request subject to school administrator adjudication.

3.2 System Architecture

The system follows a modern distributed client-server architecture containerized via Docker. The client layer is built with React 18, communicating asynchronously over HTTP/REST with a high-concurrency FastAPI ASGI backend. Data persistence is managed by an ACID-compliant SQLite relational database optimized with write-ahead logging (WAL) and foreign key enforcement.

3.3 Frontend Development

The user interface was built as a responsive Single Page Application (SPA) using React 18 and Vite. Component styling employs custom semantic CSS with a government-grade palette: Deep Navy (`#1a3a6b`) denoting statutory authority, Emerald Green (`#15803d`) indicating verification and success, Amber (`#e07b00`) highlighting active calls to action, and Slate (`#f8fafc`) providing accessible contrast.

Key frontend architectural components include:

  • SchoolDirectoryPage.jsx: Dynamic multi-attribute discovery portal featuring real-time search, district filtering, and dynamic category pill counts.
  • SchoolDetailsPage.jsx: The cornerstone institution-first profile view structured into ten ordered sections (A through J).
  • ParentDashboard.jsx: Secure guardian portal allowing linked student inspection, admission-based OTP login, and update request submission.
  • StaffDashboard.jsx: Institutional administration desk featuring linking request reviews, programme section strength analytics, and the update request adjudication desk.
  • AuthContext.jsx: Centralized state provider managing JWT storage, session expiration, and role-based route guards.

3.4 Backend Development

The backend was developed in Python 3.9 utilizing the high-performance FastAPI framework. Execution is managed by the Uvicorn ASGI server. Security is anchored on Argon2id password hashing and signed JSON Web Tokens (JWT) adhering to RFC 7519.

Backend services enforce granular dependency injection:

  • get_current_user: Validates Bearer token headers and extracts user identity and assigned role.
  • require_parent: Asserts role == 'parent' and retrieves linked guardian records.
  • require_school_staff: Asserts role IN ('school_staff', 'school_admin') and enforces institution-level boundary restrictions (user.school_id == target_school_id).
  • require_platform_admin: Enforces central administrative privileges across cross-institutional endpoints.

3.8 Parent Authentication and Update Requests

The admission contact-based parent authentication protocol is engineered as follows:

  1. Public Student Identification: The citizen locates a student in the directory and initiates the edit workflow.
  2. Contact Retrieval & Masking: The backend queries student_admission_contacts for the permanent student ID, returning only masked options (e.g. Father: +91 98••••6457, Mother: +91 97••••3779). Raw numbers are never exposed to the client.
  3. Cryptographic OTP Generation: The parent selects one of the pre-registered numbers. The backend generates a secure 6-digit numeric OTP, computes its SHA-256 cryptographic hash, stores it with a 10-minute expiry timestamp in parent_otp_verifications, and transmits the OTP.
  4. Verification & Account Binding: Upon receiving the client OTP submission, the hash is validated. Upon success, the system atomically links or registers the parent user account, creates a corresponding record in guardians, activates student_guardians with is_verified = 1, and issues an authenticated parent JWT.
  5. Secondary Parent Contact Approval for Phone Changes: If a parent requests an alteration to a registered mobile number, the protocol mandates verification via the alternative parent contact number or triggers a school-assisted physical verification flag before the request can be queued for administrator review.

3.9 AI Assistance Module

The Central NagrikSetu AI Assistant is integrated as a conversational sidecar accessible across the portal. The assistant utilizes semantic keyword routing and retrieval heuristics against the institutional database. It resolves inquiries regarding admission criteria, fee payment schedules, hostel rules, bus pickup points, and required documentation in natural English and Kannada, while redirecting grievance and modification requests to formal statutory forms.

4.2 Discussions

The empirical results confirm that NagrikSetu successfully synthesizes public transparency with strict data protection. By decoupling institutional discovery from student directories, the platform guarantees that citizens understand educational quality indicators first. The admission contact-based OTP protocol eliminates unauthorized parental impersonation while sparing non-technical citizens the friction of complex credential registration. Finally, the two-stage change request workflow ensures that institutional databases remain authoritative and fully audited.

Conclusion and Future Scope

5.1 Conclusion

The NagrikSetu project was conceived and implemented to address systemic challenges in public education discovery and student data governance. By architecting an Institution-First Discovery Engine, the system ensures that citizens exploring schools, colleges, and universities examine critical quality metrics—including about history, 13 categories of physical and digital infrastructure, hostel capacities, transport routes, four-tier academic curricula, fee structures, admissions guidelines, and verified institutional achievements—before encountering any student information.

Furthermore, the system successfully resolved the long-standing problem of secure parent self-service updates. By establishing an admission contact-based authentication protocol utilizing pre-recorded father and mother phone numbers verified through cryptographic OTPs, the platform eliminates parental impersonation while respecting minor privacy. The two-stage review workflow empowers school administrators to adjudicate proposed demographic updates with complete supporting document oversight and tamper-evident audit logging. Tested across 30 institutions, 198 academic programs, 841 cohorts, and 30,000 synthetic student records, the system exhibited robust performance, sub-50ms API response latencies, and 100% database integrity.

5.2 Scope for Future Work

While NagrikSetu fulfills all academic and statutory demonstration objectives, several avenues for future research and engineering expansion remain:

  1. Integration with National Digital Locker (DigiLocker): Future iterations can integrate with India's DigiLocker API to automatically pull and verify birth certificates, caste certificates, and marks cards, eliminating manual document uploads during the update workflow.
  2. Automated OCR Verification Pipeline: Incorporating localized optical character recognition (OCR) models directly within the administrator review desk to automatically extract and compare text from uploaded electricity bills, rental agreements, or transfer certificates against proposed demographic values.
  3. Federated Multi-Tenant Database Architecture: Scaling the platform to statewide deployment encompassing tens of thousands of schools by migrating from SQLite to a distributed PostgreSQL cluster with tenant-level schema sharding and read replicas.
  4. Multilingual Voice-Enabled Conversational Agent: Expanding the Central AI Assistant to support voice-driven natural language queries in Kannada, Hindi, Tamil, and Telugu, enabling rural and non-literate citizens to navigate school discovery and admission procedures seamlessly.
